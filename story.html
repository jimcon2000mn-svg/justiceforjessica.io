Jessica's Story

Jessica was 18 years old when her infant son, Corbin, died in December 2003. What followed changed the course of her life and ultimately led to her incarceration.

The following account is based on Jessica's recollection of the events surrounding Corbin's death, the investigation, and the events that led to her arrest.

The Days Before Corbin's Death

On December 29, 2003, Jessica left her son Corbin with her husband, Dwayne. When she returned approximately an hour later, Dwayne told her that Corbin had stopped breathing.

Jessica examined Corbin and believed that he appeared to be fine, but she contacted his pediatrician. At the doctor's appointment, Corbin was diagnosed with bronchitis, thrush, and an ear infection. Jessica was instructed to begin treatments at home.

The following day, Jessica was caring for her son as well as two children belonging to a friend. Later that afternoon, Dwayne returned home. Jessica prepared to leave to pick up her friend's mother from work.

The Emergency

Before leaving, Jessica placed Corbin in a reclining chair, propped up with blankets and a stuffed animal, and told Dwayne she would be back.

While Jessica was away, Dwayne was alone with Corbin. According to Dwayne's account at the time, he later discovered that Corbin was not breathing and called 911.

On her way home, Jessica saw an ambulance traveling from the direction of her house. When she arrived, police and volunteer firefighters were at the residence. She was told that Corbin had stopped breathing and had been taken to the hospital.

Jessica immediately went to the hospital. Doctors attempted to resuscitate Corbin, but they were unable to save him. Jessica was allowed to say goodbye to her son.

The Investigation

In the aftermath of Corbin's death, Jessica says there was considerable confusion about what had happened. Her family questioned whether Corbin's medical condition had been properly diagnosed.

Police and Child Protective Services questioned Jessica and Dwayne separately. Jessica was also questioned about her life, her marriage, and the circumstances surrounding Corbin's death.

According to Jessica, a medical malpractice attorney was initially contacted because of concerns about Corbin's medical treatment and the number of medications he had received.

Jessica says that during meetings with the attorney, Dwayne gave different accounts about what he had done while alone with Corbin.

Dwayne's Arrest

On January 6, 2004, investigators came to Jessica's home. Dwayne was arrested and charged in connection with Corbin's death.

Jessica was told that Corbin's autopsy had indicated blunt force trauma to the head and that investigators believed Dwayne was responsible.

Jessica says that she initially had difficulty accepting that Dwayne could have caused Corbin's injuries. She told investigators that she did not believe he had done it. According to her account, she was confused and overwhelmed by the circumstances surrounding her son's death.

A Time of Crisis

Following Dwayne's arrest, Jessica says her life began to unravel. She lost her home, moved between the homes of friends and family, and struggled with intense grief.

During this period, Jessica says she began abusing alcohol and drugs. She describes experiencing hallucinations and believing things that were not reality.

According to Jessica, Dwayne repeatedly contacted her from jail and told her that he had not caused Corbin's death. She says he also pressured her to find a way to get him released.

The Confession

In February 2004, while in an extremely unstable state, Jessica went to the police department and told officers that she wanted to confess to Corbin's death.

According to Jessica, she was instructed to write a detailed confession. She wrote that she might have placed Corbin on the couch too hard, causing the blunt force trauma described by investigators.

Jessica says that she immediately knew the confession was not true. She maintains that she gave the confession while experiencing severe emotional distress and the effects of drug use.

After leaving the police department, Jessica traveled to Mt. Pleasant. Her behavior became increasingly disorganized, and police eventually took her to a hospital.

Hospitalization and Arrest

Jessica was admitted to the hospital and underwent a toxicology screening. While hospitalized, she says she contacted people she knew and told them that the confession she had given was false.

Jessica was released from the hospital on February 17, 2004, and was instructed to seek mental-health and grief counseling.

According to her account, she went to the courthouse shortly afterward to address her personal-recognizance bond. While she was there, she was arrested for the death of her son.

More Than 20 Years

More than two decades have passed since the events that changed Jessica's life.

Jessica maintains that she did not cause her son's death and that the confession she gave was false. Her account raises questions about the circumstances surrounding the confession and the events leading to her conviction.

This website was created to share Jessica's story and to provide information about the ongoing effort to obtain legal assistance and pursue available avenues for her case.

Evidence the Jury Never Heard

Those advocating for Jessica say that important evidence was not presented to the jury. They believe this information deserves careful review because it may affect how the circumstances surrounding Jessica's confession and Corbin's death are understood.

The Recorded Phone Call

According to Jessica's supporters, the district attorney's office possesses recordings of telephone calls made by inmates. They say one recording captures a conversation between Dwayne and Jessica in which Dwayne allegedly asked Jessica to give a false confession to help get him out of jail.

Her supporters say the recording was in the evidence but was not played for the jury. If the recording confirms this account, it could be important to understanding the circumstances surrounding Jessica's confession.

Statements Made While Jessica Was in the ICU

Another concern involves testimony from a state witness about a conversation with Jessica while Jessica was in the intensive care unit (ICU). According to the account provided by her supporters, Jessica was hospitalized with drug-induced psychosis at the time.

Her supporters question whether statements attributed to Jessica during this period should have been admitted at trial, given her reported medical condition. They believe this testimony was damaging to her defense and that the circumstances in which the statements were made deserved close examination.

Questions About the Dates on the Confession

Jessica's supporters have also raised concerns about an officer's testimony under oath regarding the dates written on the confession. They allege that the dates described in the testimony were inaccurate and believe the trial testimony and related records should be carefully reviewed.

A Defense Witness Who Was Not Called

Her supporters also say that a key defense witness was not called to testify. According to their account, this witness could have testified that Corbin was alive and well when Jessica and the witness left Dwayne alone with Corbin at the house.

Jessica's supporters believe this testimony could have helped the jury understand the timeline and circumstances surrounding Corbin's death.

Questions That Deserve Review

These are among the concerns raised by those seeking justice for Jessica and Corbin. They believe the recorded phone call, the circumstances surrounding Jessica's statements in the ICU, the disputed testimony about the confession's dates, and the absence of the defense witness warrant serious examination.

This website shares these concerns to encourage a careful review of the available records and evidence. The underlying recordings, medical records, trial transcript, and other court documents are important for assessing what happened and what legal options may remain.

The Fight for Justice

Jessica's story is about more than a criminal case. It is also about a young mother who experienced an unimaginable loss and the events that followed.

After more than 20 years of incarceration, Jessica and those supporting her are seeking legal assistance to examine her case and pursue the available legal options.
